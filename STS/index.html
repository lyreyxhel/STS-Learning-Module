Course topics

STS connects innovation with everyday life

Science, Technology, and Society looks at how knowledge and inventions shape communities, and how human needs, public choices, ethics, and access shape technology in return. Explore the topics from our course materials below.

The big picture

Science builds knowledge. Technology puts it to work.

Society influences which questions researchers study, what problems receive attention, and which technologies are designed and funded. These priorities can reflect public needs, cultural values, business interests, government policies, and available resources. In turn, scientific discoveries and new tools can reshape health care, education, work, communication, the economy, and the environment. These changes may improve daily life, but their benefits and costs are not always shared equally. Some communities may face limited access, job disruption, privacy concerns, or environmental harm. STS helps us examine these effects, ask who benefits and who is left out, and consider what ethical responsibilities should guide the development and use of technology.

01 · Nation building

Building a stronger Philippines

Research helps explain local needs; technology applies that knowledge in areas such as health, agriculture, education, transport, and communication. Public policy and investment affect which ideas reach people.

Consider the role of DOST, science scholarships, technology transfer, and startup support—and challenges such as limited research funding, brain drain, and unequal access.

02 · Information age

Living in an information society

Digital networks make it possible to create, find, share, and use information quickly. They open new ways to learn and communicate, while raising questions about access, the digital divide, and how people use information responsibly.

03 · Technology and humanity

Human dignity and rights

The Universal Declaration of Human Rights centers dignity and equal rights. When technology changes social life, ask whether its design and use protect people, treat them fairly, and support their well-being.

▶ Watch UNESCO video
04 · Gene therapy

Medicine, possibility, and ethics

Gene therapy may replace or switch off a faulty gene, or add a gene to help fight disease. Somatic cell therapy affects the patient; changes to germ cells can be inherited.

Potential benefits come with questions about safety, lasting effects, cost and access, how society defines a disorder, and whether gene editing should be used for enhancement.

05 · Industrial revolutions

Four waves of change

  • IR 1: steam power and mechanized production
  • IR 2: electricity and mass production
  • IR 3: computers, the internet, and automation
  • IR 4: artificial intelligence, connected devices, and data

Each wave changed jobs and daily life, while creating new effects on the environment. Think about who gains, who must adapt, and how innovation can be sustainable.
